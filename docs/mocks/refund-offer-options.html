Refund offers

An admin offers a refund. The buyer gets an email with the admin's own message and a link. To take it, they confirm their email with a code, then the refund goes through Stripe and their tickets stop working. Nobody can claim a refund that was not offered.

How it works

Admin offersPicks the amount, writes a message, sets how long it stays open.
Buyer is emailedThe message, the amount, and a "Get my refund" button.
Buyer confirmsA 6-digit code goes to the order's email. Forwarded links and friends holding a ticket can't claim it.
Refund goes throughStripe refunds the card, the tickets are voided, the seats go back on sale, the usual refund email follows.

Pick one admin option and one buyer option. They mix freely; the email is the same either way.

Decisions for you

  1. Admin A, B, or both?I'd build B with A inside it: the same dialog, from one order or the whole event. A date change is when this gets used most.
  2. Buyer A or B?I lean B: one place for everything about their order, and it already shows the receipt. A is clearer if you expect people to share ticket links around.
  3. The fee.The mocks offer "everything" or "tickets only" per offer. If "fee refund" meant something else, like a refund with a fee taken off, tell me and the amount picker changes.
  4. All or nothing per order?The mocks refund the whole order. Letting a buyer refund 1 of their 3 tickets is possible, but adds a step.
  5. How long an offer stays open.Default 14 days, editable, and never past the event's start.
