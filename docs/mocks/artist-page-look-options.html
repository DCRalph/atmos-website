A better looking artist page

Editing works now. This is about how the page looks once it's edited. Every part below is shown on @probablysunday's real content: today's screenshot first, then mockups of new options. Each part has a recommendation, and they add up to the full page in part 6.

1

Where it is today

Sunday's page uses the Headliner layout with the default theme and a red accent. The banner is a 640px wide photo of an empty car park, the avatar is a logo, and most of the page is one long centred text block beside four embeds.

  1. The name sits low on the left with a dead band of dark photo above it. A 640px banner stretched to 950px is soft, and the scrims turn it to mud.
  2. The Recently rail is a flat grey slab with a hard seam against the photo. It doesn't belong to the photo or to the page below.
  3. Socials, the Listen pill and the rail's View labels are three different kinds of control, spread across the header with nothing tying them together.
  1. The heading block is set enormous, then the text block repeats the idea with centred BIO: and INSPIRATIONS: headings in a second style.
  2. A 5,000 character bio, centred, as one wall. Nothing folds it away.
  3. Each embed brings its own look (a white Cygnus card, Spotify's grey, SoundCloud's orange) with nothing framing or naming them.
  4. Blocks sit 24px apart with no surface, so the grid reads as a pile rather than a layout.
  1. A full-height black portrait box holding a small logo, for a one-line bio.
  2. Then the name again, huge, faint and cropped, before the footer.

2

The header

Three directions. All of them work with what artists actually upload: a mood photo instead of a portrait, a logo as an avatar, a low resolution banner. All of them take the theme's ground, accent, face and corners.

A · Cover

Recommended

The banner becomes atmosphere (darkened, tinted with the accent, fading into the page) so a soft or moody photo still looks deliberate. The avatar sits in a framed square like a record sleeve, so a logo works as well as a face. The name, what they do and every action sit together in one block: Listen jumps to their music, and a page with a Links block gets its first link as a second button. The grey rail becomes a glass strip of recent sets along the bottom of the photo.

3

Blocks

Two ways to give every block the same look. Both are drawn with Sunday's real layout: heading, bio and socials on the left, release, Spotify, SoundCloud and video on the right.

Tiles

Recommended

Each block is a raised tile with the theme's corners and a small label naming what it is (Bio, Listen · Spotify, Watch). Embeds sit inside a frame, so Spotify's grey and SoundCloud's orange read as content rather than as clashing chrome. The grid becomes a visible bento rather than a pile. Headings stay bare, as section titles.

Bio

Sunday is a New Zealand based producer, crafting bass-heavy dance music that thrives on the weird, the wonderful, and the unexpected. Blending weighty low-end with left-field textures, warped grooves and high-energy club rhythms, Sunday pushes UK-inspired sounds into fresh territory while keeping one foot firmly on the dancefloor.

Already earning support from the likes of Notion, Hermitude, Burt Cope, MSFT, Jeremy Sylvester and many more. Sunday’s music is finding its way into sets from some of the scene’s most respected selectors.

Inspirations

As I’ve developed my appreciation for dance music over the years, I’ve discovered countless artists and DJs who have inspired me creatively. However, one artist who stands out above many others is Bakey. There is something about his music that resonates with me in a way few others have managed to achieve.

Still easy to theme

Every colour, face and corner above comes from the five theme choices. The same page in two other starters:

8

What it takes

  • Header A replaces the Headliner hero: the banner treatment, the framed avatar, one action row, and the sets strip in place of the rail. Wall and Stage get the same tidy-up for their headers.
  • Tiles are one wrapper in the shared block cell, used by the public page and the editor alike, plus a label per block type. Corners, surface and label colour come from the theme.
  • Heading sizes, the text fold and the player settings are small changes to each block's renderer.
  • Release is a new block type: cover upload, title, label and a list of store links.
  • About and the closing band are layout changes. A short bio moves into the header.
  • No database changes apart from the new Release block's data.
